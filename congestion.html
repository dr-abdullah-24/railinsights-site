How to read this analysis & data limitations

Clustering: delayed services are sorted by lateness, then grouped within 3 km of each unassigned seed. Groups need at least three services; each service belongs to one group. Geographic proximity can span separate railways and does not demonstrate a queue.

Metrics: cancelled services and positions older than the chosen limit are excluded. Only delay reports up to 15 minutes old with a recognised TRUST status are counted. Unknown delay is never treated as on time. Summed lateness is a snapshot, not cumulative or attributed delay. Filters apply to all metrics, clusters, map and export.

Location confidence: positions come from the existing TD berth lookup, not GPS. Several berths may share coordinates; the bridge can retain a previous coordinate for an unmapped berth. Headcodes can be reused, and the current bridge joins by headcode. Validate service identity before operational use.

Capacity and causality: this feed cannot establish capacity utilisation, signal headways, platform conflicts, root cause, or predicted recovery. These require topology, schedules, occupation history and incident attribution. Read Network Rail’s bottleneck explanation and knock-on delay guidance.

Imagery: the Esri World Imagery mosaic provides geographic context only. It cannot confirm present train positions or incidents. Sentinel-2 has a multi-day revisit interval rather than continuous video.
